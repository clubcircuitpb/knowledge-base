**The short version:** serve underhand and diagonally, past the kitchen. Each team serves until it loses a rally; there is no second server. The return of serve and the next shot must both bounce before anyone volleys.

These pages summarize the USA Pickleball rules players need most, in our own words. Where ClubCircuit has its own rule, the ClubCircuit rule applies.

## How do I hit a legal serve?

You can use either a volley serve or a drop serve.

|  | Volley serve | Drop serve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it starts | You hit the ball out of the air | You drop the ball and hit it after it bounces |
| Swing | Paddle moving in a clear upward arc | Any swing |
| Contact height | Ball clearly below your waist; paddle head clearly not above your wrist | Any height |
| Release | Let go of the ball without spinning it | Drop it; don't toss it up or throw it down, and don't spin it |

For both serves:

- You may put spin on the ball with your paddle as you hit it, but not with your hand or paddle as you release it.
- At least one foot must be behind the baseline. Neither foot may touch the court, the baseline, or the area outside the extended sideline and centerline.
- Serve diagonally to the opponent's service court. The ball must clear the kitchen; landing on the kitchen line is short and a fault. Landing on any other line of the service court is in.
- A serve that clips the net and lands in the right court is in play. You get one serve per rally.

## Who serves, and from which side?

Under rally scoring there is no second server:

1. **One serve turn per team.** Your team keeps serving until it loses a rally. Then the other team gets the point and the serve.
2. **The same player keeps serving.** Each time your team wins a rally on serve, the server and partner swap sides and the same player serves again from the other side.
3. **When you win the serve back,** the player standing in the right-hand court serves.
4. **Positions follow your team's score.** The player who served first for your team stands on the right when your score is even (0, 2, 4...) and on the left when it's odd. This applies whether you're serving or receiving.

Stacking is allowed: partners may stand wherever they like, as long as the correct player serves and the correct player receives.

## How do I call the score?

The server calls the score before every serve: serving team's score first, then the receiving team's. There's no server number in rally scoring.

<Note>
  **Example:** your team has 8 and the other team has 11. You call "8–11" before serving.
</Note>

## What if the wrong score is called?

- **Before the return of serve:** any player may stop play to correct it. The rally is replayed with the right score.
- **After the return of serve:** play on. Fix the score before the next serve; the rally result stands.
- **Stopping play late, or when the score was right:** the player who stopped play loses the rally.

## What is the two-bounce rule?

The receiving team must let the serve bounce before hitting it. The serving team must then let the return bounce before hitting it. After those two bounces, either team may volley (hit the ball out of the air) or let it bounce.

<Warning>
  **Costs the rally:** volleying the serve or the return of serve.
</Warning>

## What else ends a rally?

A rally ends when a team faults. Under rally scoring, the other team wins the point. The [kitchen, faults and line calls](/rules/rally-21/kitchen-faults-and-line-calls) page has the full list.
