**The short version:** don't volley while touching the kitchen. Call lines on your own side, and if you're not sure a ball was out, it's in. Any fault loses the rally, and under rally scoring the other team gets the point.

## What is the kitchen?

The kitchen, officially the non-volley zone, is the area within 7 feet of the net on each side. Its lines are part of it.

## What can't I do in the kitchen?

You can't volley (hit the ball before it bounces) while touching the kitchen or its line. That also covers:

- **Momentum.** If your follow-through carries you, or anything you're wearing or holding, into the kitchen after a volley, it's a fault, even if the rally is already over.
- **Your gear.** Your paddle, hat or anything else that touches the kitchen during a volley counts as you touching it.
- **Your partner.** If you volley while touching your partner and your partner is touching the kitchen, it's a fault.
- **Re-entering.** After stepping into the kitchen, both feet must be back outside it before you can volley.

## Can I stand in the kitchen?

Yes, at any time. You can also step in to hit a ball that has bounced.

<Warning>
  **Costs the rally:** volleying while touching the kitchen or its line, or being carried into it by your volley.
</Warning>

## What counts as a fault?

Your team loses the rally if a player on it:

| Fault | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Hits the ball out | Lands outside the court lines. A ball touching any line is in, except the kitchen line on a serve. |
| Hits into the net | The ball doesn't make it over. |
| Lets the ball bounce twice | You must return it before its second bounce on your side. |
| Is hit by the ball | The ball hits you or your clothing before it bounces, even if you're standing out of bounds. Your paddle hand below the wrist counts as part of the paddle. |
| Double-hits | The same player hits the ball twice in a stroke that isn't one continuous motion in one direction. |
| Two-player hit | You and your partner both touch the ball. |
| Touches the net | Your body, paddle or clothing touches the net or net post while the ball is in play. |
| Reaches over the net | You hit the ball before it has crossed to your side. Following through over the net after a legal hit is fine. |
| Breaks the two-bounce rule | You volley the serve or the return of serve. |
| Volleys in the kitchen | See above. |
| Distracts an opponent | You deliberately shout, stomp or make a gesture to throw them off. |
| Drops or shows a spare ball | An extra ball you're carrying falls on the court, or your opponent can see it, during a rally. |

## Who calls the lines?

You call the lines on your side of the net. Your opponents call theirs. Off-court players and spectators may not make or influence calls.

## What if I'm not sure whether it was out?

It's in. Only call a ball out if you clearly saw space between the ball and the line. If you didn't see it, you can ask your opponents; if they didn't see it clearly either, it's in.

## What if my partner and I disagree?

If one partner calls it out and the other thinks it was in, the ball is in.

## When do I have to call it out?

Right away, out loud, a hand signal, or both:

- **If you hit the ball back,** call it out before your opponent hits it or the rally ends. Otherwise play continues.
- **If you let it go,** call it promptly, even if the ball has already stopped.

Shouting "out" to your partner while the ball is in the air is advice, not a line call.

## When is a point replayed?

- A ball from another court rolls onto yours, or something else outside the game gets in the way. Call it right away and replay the rally.
- A foot-fault call is disputed (see [On match day](/rules/rally-21/on-match-day)).
- A wrong score is caught before the return of serve (see [Serving and rallies](/rules/rally-21/serving-and-rallies)).
