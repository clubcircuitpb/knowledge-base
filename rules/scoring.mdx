Accurate scoring is the foundation of fair competition. In Club Circuit leagues, every player on the court shares responsibility for tracking the score, calling it correctly before each serve, and resolving any disagreements promptly and honestly. This section explains how points are earned, how the score is announced, what constitutes a fault, and how to handle timeouts and score disputes during a match.

## Rally Scoring

All Club Circuit leagues use **rally scoring** by default. Under rally scoring, a point is awarded on every rally — regardless of which team served. This differs from traditional (side-out) scoring, where only the serving team can score a point.

Rally scoring keeps games moving at a consistent pace and makes every point equally important. If your league organizer has enabled traditional scoring as a custom rule, that will be noted in your league dashboard.

## How a Point Is Scored

<Steps>
  <Step title="The server initiates play">
    The server calls the score, then serves the ball diagonally into the correct service box. The serve must clear the non-volley zone (kitchen) and land in bounds.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The rally is played">
    Both teams hit the ball back and forth until the rally ends. A rally ends when the ball bounces twice, goes out of bounds, hits the net without clearing it, or a fault is committed by any player.
  </Step>
  <Step title="A fault is committed">
    The team that commits the fault loses the rally. The opposing team is awarded the point.
  </Step>
  <Step title="The point is recorded">
    The team that won the rally receives one point. If the serving team won the rally, they retain the serve. If the receiving team won the rally, they earn the point **and** the serve (a side-out).
  </Step>
  <Step title="The score is updated and play continues">
    Both teams acknowledge the new score. The new server calls the updated score aloud before the next serve.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Calling the Score

Before every serve, the server must call the score in the following three-number format:

> **Server score – Receiver score – Server number**

For example, **"5 – 3 – 2"** means: the serving team has 5 points, the receiving team has 3 points, and the second player on the serving team is currently serving.

- **Server number** is either 1 or 2, identifying which doubles partner is serving.
- At the start of each game, the first serving team uses **server number 2** (the "first server" exception), ensuring the opposing team gets an early opportunity to serve.
- In singles, omit the server number — the format is simply **Server score – Receiver score**.

Calling the score correctly and audibly before every serve is not a courtesy — it is a rule. If a serve is delivered without a score call and an opponent questions the score, replay the point.

<Tip>
  Make it a habit to call the score loudly and clearly before every single serve, even when the score seems obvious. This simple practice prevents the vast majority of score disputes during a match and keeps the game moving smoothly.
</Tip>

## Fault Rules

A **fault** ends the rally and awards the point to the non-faulting team. The following actions result in a fault:

- **Foot faults:** The server steps on or over the baseline, or lands outside the correct service box area, during the serve.
- **Kitchen violations (Non-Volley Zone faults):** A player volleys the ball (hits it before it bounces) while standing in the kitchen, or their momentum carries them into the kitchen immediately after a volley.
- **Out of bounds:** The ball lands outside the court boundary lines. The line itself is considered in bounds — a ball touching any part of the line is in.
- **Double bounce violation:** A team fails to let the ball bounce once on their side during the first two shots of a rally (the two-bounce rule).
- **Net violations:** The ball hits the net post, or a player or their paddle contacts the net during a rally.
- **Serve faults:** The serve lands in the kitchen, fails to clear the net, or lands out of the correct service area.

When you observe a fault by your opponent, call it promptly and clearly. Do not wait until after the rally to retroactively call a fault that you saw during play.

## Timeouts

Each team is allowed **two timeouts per game**. Timeouts may be called by any player on the court at any time between rallies — never during an active rally.

- Each timeout lasts a maximum of **60 seconds**.
- Unused timeouts do not carry over between games.
- A team that has used both timeouts may not request an additional timeout. Requesting an unauthorized timeout is a fault.
- Medical timeouts are handled separately and are granted at the discretion of the referee or, in non-officiated matches, by mutual agreement of both teams.

To call a timeout, clearly say "Timeout" before the next serve begins. Both teams should acknowledge the timeout before either team moves off their positions.

## Dispute Resolution

Score disagreements happen, even in matches played in good faith. Use the following procedure to resolve them quickly:

1. **Stop play immediately** when a score dispute is raised. Do not continue serving while the dispute is unresolved.
2. **Both teams state the score** they believe to be correct and explain why.
3. **Agree on the last indisputable score** — the last point both teams remember without disagreement — and use that as the basis for reconciliation.
4. **If agreement cannot be reached**, the point in question is replayed from the last agreed score. No points are awarded or removed beyond the replay point.
5. **In officiated matches**, refer all unresolved disputes to the referee. The referee's decision is final.

Repeated score disputes caused by a player's failure to call the score before each serve may result in a warning from the referee or league organizer.
