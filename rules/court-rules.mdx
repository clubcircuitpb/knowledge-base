Understanding the court is the foundation of fair and competitive play. Club Circuit enforces standard USA Pickleball (USAPA) court specifications across all league matches. Whether you are playing at a league-provided facility or a self-arranged court, you are responsible for knowing these rules and playing within them. Disputes that arise from misunderstanding court boundaries or zone restrictions will be resolved according to the guidelines on this page.

## Standard Court Dimensions

All Club Circuit league matches are played on regulation pickleball courts measuring **20 feet wide by 44 feet long** — the same dimensions for both singles and doubles play.

| Measurement | Specification |
|---|---|
| Court width | 20 feet |
| Court length | 44 feet |
| Net height at center | 34 inches |
| Net height at sideposts | 36 inches |
| Non-volley zone depth | 7 feet from net on each side |
| Service area width | 10 feet |

The net must be taut and secured at the correct heights before play begins. If you notice a net that is improperly set up before the match starts, notify your opponent and correct it before serving. Do not begin a match on a net you know to be out of specification.

<Note>
  Net posts must be positioned outside the court boundaries. If a ball strikes a net post, it is a fault — the point is replayed only if the ball would otherwise have been in play.
</Note>

## Non-Volley Zone (Kitchen) Rules

The non-volley zone — commonly called the **kitchen** — extends 7 feet from the net on both sides of the court, spanning the full 20-foot width. The kitchen line itself is part of the non-volley zone.

### What you cannot do in the kitchen

- **Volley the ball** — you may not hit the ball out of the air while any part of your body, clothing, or paddle is touching the non-volley zone or its lines.
- **Enter the kitchen as a result of your volley** — if your momentum carries you into the kitchen after striking a volley, it is a fault even if you have already made contact with the ball.
- **Let your partner's momentum push you into the kitchen** — if physical contact with your partner causes you to enter the NVZ after a volley, that is also a fault.

### What you can do in the kitchen

- Step into the kitchen to play a ball that has **bounced** inside the zone — this is legal and common.
- Stand in the kitchen at any time when you are **not volleying**.
- Return to the kitchen after a volley attempt, provided your momentum from that volley did not carry you in.

<Tip>
  Many faults occur because players forget that the kitchen line itself is part of the non-volley zone. Keep your feet clearly behind the line when volleying near the net.
</Tip>

## Line Rules

Line calls are a routine part of pickleball, and Club Circuit follows USAPA standards for all boundary decisions.

- **A ball that lands on any court line is considered in**, with one exception.
- **A serve that lands on the kitchen line is a fault** — the serve must clear the non-volley zone entirely and land in the appropriate service box. Contact with the kitchen line on a serve results in a side-out or re-serve, depending on the score situation.
- **Out-of-bounds calls** should be made promptly, clearly, and honestly. If you are not certain a ball is out, you must call it in.
- In the absence of a line judge, each team is responsible for making line calls on their own side of the court.

<Warning>
  Intentionally making incorrect line calls is a conduct violation. Repeated questionable calls may be grounds for a formal dispute. Call what you see — if in doubt, play it in.
</Warning>

## Facility and Court Etiquette

Respectful court behavior is required at all Club Circuit venues. The following standards apply whether you are on a league-provided court or a self-arranged facility.

### During active play on adjacent courts

- **Wait for a point to complete** before crossing behind or alongside an active court. Never walk through a live point.
- If a ball rolls onto your court from an adjacent game, stop play immediately, return the ball, and replay the point.
- Keep noise levels considerate — excessive celebrating or arguing that disrupts neighboring games may result in a conduct warning.

### Sideline and court area conduct

- Keep all bags, equipment, and personal items off the court surface and well clear of the sidelines.
- Spectators and waiting players must remain behind the designated waiting areas — not on the court or directly on the sidelines during a live match.
- Remove all food and drinks from the court surface. Spills create safety hazards and may result in your team forfeiting court time.

## Warm-Up and Court Rotation

<Tip>
  Arrive at least **10 minutes before your scheduled match time** to warm up. Each team is allotted equal warm-up time on the court — typically 3 to 5 minutes per side depending on the facility schedule. Do not use your warm-up time to practice serves exclusively; allow your opponent equal rallying time at the net.
</Tip>

When courts are shared across multiple matches in a session, follow the rotation schedule posted by the facility or your league administrator. Holding a court beyond your allotted match time — even if your match is still in progress — may result in the match being paused and rescheduled. Check your division's schedule in the Club Circuit platform for specific court time windows.
