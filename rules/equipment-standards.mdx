Every player competing in a Club Circuit league match is responsible for ensuring their equipment meets official standards before stepping on the court. Club Circuit follows USA Pickleball Association (USAPA) equipment regulations for all sanctioned play. Using non-compliant equipment — whether intentionally or by oversight — can result in forfeiture and may affect your team's standing. Review these standards before each season and whenever you purchase new gear.

## Approved Paddles

All paddles used in Club Circuit league matches must appear on the **USAPA Approved Paddle List** at the time of play. It is your responsibility to verify approval status before purchasing or playing with a new paddle.

### Size and surface requirements

| Specification | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Combined length + width | Must not exceed 24 inches |
| Maximum length | 17 inches |
| Surface texture | No rough, abrasive, or adhesive materials |
| Core material | Any — composite, graphite, polymer, and wood cores are all permitted |
| Edge guard | Allowed; must not add significant hitting surface |
| Anti-skid paint or coatings | Prohibited |

### Additional paddle rules

- Paddle surfaces must be **free of delamination, cracks, or damage** that could affect ball response. A damaged paddle that produces inconsistent ball behavior may be ruled illegal mid-match.
- Stickers, decals, and custom artwork are permitted **only on the paddle face and handle** — not applied over the hitting surface in a way that alters texture.
- Built-in electronic devices, including vibration dampeners with embedded sensors, are not permitted unless explicitly approved by USAPA.

<Note>
  Before purchasing a new paddle, check the [USAPA Equipment Approved List](https://usapickleball.org/play/equipment-standards/approved-paddles/) directly. Approval status can change — a paddle approved last season may be removed from the list.
</Note>

## Ball Standards

### Outdoor vs. indoor balls

Club Circuit matches are played with balls appropriate to the court surface. Outdoor and indoor balls are not interchangeable.

**Outdoor Balls:** Harder plastic construction with smaller, more numerous holes (typically 40 holes). Designed to resist wind and perform consistently on harder court surfaces. Required for all outdoor league play.

**Indoor Balls:** Softer plastic with larger, fewer holes (typically 26 holes). Designed for gym floors and smoother indoor surfaces. Required for all indoor league play.

### Approved manufacturers

Club Circuit recommends — and in tournament-style divisions, requires — balls from USAPA-approved manufacturers. Commonly used approved balls include:

- **Dura Fast 40** (outdoor)
- **Onix Fuse G2** (outdoor)
- **Jugs Indoor Pickleball** (indoor)
- **Onix Pure 2** (indoor)

Your league administrator will specify the approved ball for your division at the start of each season. In recreational divisions, either team may supply a USAPA-approved ball with mutual agreement.

<Warning>
  Do not begin play with a cracked or visibly damaged ball. If a ball cracks during a point, that point is replayed. Continuing to play with a known-damaged ball after it is identified is a fault on the team that declined to replace it.
</Warning>

## Attire Guidelines

Athletic clothing appropriate for court sport is required at all Club Circuit matches. You are expected to appear in clean, presentable attire that allows full range of motion.

- **Acceptable:** Athletic shorts, skirts, skorts, leggings, athletic shirts, moisture-wicking polos.
- **Not acceptable:** Jeans, cargo pants, open-toed shoes, dress shoes, or clothing with offensive graphics or language.
- **All-white attire is prohibited** — wearing entirely white clothing makes the ball difficult to track for both players and observers. At least one visible non-white element is required on your top.

<Tip>
  Bring a change of shirt to matches, especially during warmer months. A dry shirt at the start of a second game improves grip and comfort — and is a sign of professional preparation.
</Tip>

## Footwear Requirements

You must wear **non-marking court shoes** for all Club Circuit matches played on indoor courts. Shoes with black rubber soles or outsoles that leave marks are not permitted on indoor surfaces and may result in you forfeiting your court time.

For outdoor courts, court shoes or tennis shoes with lateral support are strongly recommended. Running shoes are not designed for the lateral movement of pickleball and increase your risk of injury — Club Circuit does not prohibit them, but strongly advises against them.

- **Required:** Non-marking soles on all indoor courts
- **Recommended:** Court-specific or tennis shoes for all surfaces
- **Prohibited:** Sandals, flip-flops, dress shoes, and cleats on all surfaces

## Equipment Violations

<Warning>
  Using illegal or non-approved equipment in a Club Circuit match may result in an **immediate forfeiture of the match**. If an equipment violation is identified before the match begins, you will be given the opportunity to replace the equipment. If a violation is discovered mid-match, the match result may be reviewed by the league administrator.
</Warning>

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Are grip tape and overgrips allowed?">
    Yes — grip tape and overgrips are permitted as long as they do not extend onto the paddle face or hitting surface. Standard overgrips applied to the handle only are fully legal and widely used. Make sure the grip does not add material beyond the handle area in a way that could alter how you contact the ball.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I use a paddle with a damaged surface?">
    No. A paddle with a cracked face, delaminated surface, or compromised edge guard is not legal for league play. If your opponent or a league official identifies a damaged paddle before or during your match, you must replace it before continuing. Attempting to play on with a known-damaged paddle may result in a fault or match forfeiture.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens if my opponent questions my equipment?">
    Either player may raise an equipment concern before the match begins or between games. If there is a dispute about whether a paddle is USAPA-approved, play should be paused and the issue escalated to your league administrator. In the absence of an official, take note of the paddle model and submit a formal equipment dispute through the Club Circuit platform after the match. Do not refuse to play — proceed under protest and file the dispute promptly.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
